The article, based on a Pew Research Center analysis of U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement data, reveals that the number of new foreign students with F - 1 visas enrolled at U.S. colleges and universities nearly doubled from the start of the Great Recession to 2016, increasing 104% compared to a 3.4% growth in overall college enrollment during the same period. Public institutions, facing budget cuts during the recession, saw new foreign student enrollment grow faster (107% from 2008 - 2016) than private schools (98%). New foreign students' estimated spending on U.S. higher education rose by 184% from 2008 to 2016. Graduate degrees were the most common among newly - enrolled foreign students in 2016. Students from China, India, and South Korea made up over half of new foreign students, and ten states accounted for nearly two - thirds of them in 2016. A recent survey also indicates a 7% decline in newly - enrolled international students at 500 colleges for the 2017 - 18 academic year.
