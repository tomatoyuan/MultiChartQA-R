As the 2022 campaign nears its end, a Pew Research Center analysis of over 8,000 candidate Twitter accounts shows that the online campaign is in full swing, with candidates at all government levels sharing nearly 3.4 million tweets since the start of the year. Key themes include that one - in - five tweets mention race, abortion, education, or the economy; Democrats produce most tweets on climate change and abortion, while Republicans focus on immigration; there are partisan differences in language use and tone on various topics, with Republicans more likely to use negative language; the discussion of different topics has fluctuated over time, with events like the overturn of Roe v. Wade and school shootings leading to spikes; as Election Day approaches, partisan opposition is more common in tweets; candidates frequently mention Joe Biden and Donald Trump; mentions of bipartisanship are rare; candidates in California and Texas contribute significantly to climate change and immigration discussions respectively; and nearly two - thirds of tweets do not mention the 16 analyzed issues, likely focusing on general campaign support.
