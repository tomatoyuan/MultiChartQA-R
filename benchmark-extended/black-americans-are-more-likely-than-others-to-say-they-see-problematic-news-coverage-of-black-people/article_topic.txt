The article, based on Pew Research Center surveys, explores Black Americans' experiences with news coverage of Black people. It reveals that about four - in - ten Black Americans often see racist or racially insensitive news coverage of Black people, a much higher proportion than other racial and ethnic groups like White, Asian, and Hispanic Americans. There are also political differences, with Democrats and Democratic - leaning independents more likely than Republicans to say they see such problematic coverage. Across racial and ethnic groups, majorities of Americans don't consider it important for news to come from journalists who share their political, religious, or demographic traits, but Black Americans are more likely to want news from journalists similar to them (except in sharing political views). Among Black Americans, those who often see racist news coverage are more likely to care about journalists' shared characteristics, yet a majority of Black Americans still say it's not too important to get news from Black journalists, instead emphasizing other trust - building factors in news stories.
