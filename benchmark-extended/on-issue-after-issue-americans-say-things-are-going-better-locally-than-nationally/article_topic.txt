The article discusses a common pattern in survey research where Americans express more positive views about local conditions than national ones across various issues. This is evident in election administration, with more voters saying local elections are run very well compared to national ones. Similar trends are seen in views of local and federal governments, education (where local public schools are rated more highly), news media (higher trust in local media), crime and policing (less concern about local crime), and broader assessments of the community and the country. The reasons behind this pattern are not fully clear, but it could be due to people having some control over their living location and personal experiences shaping views on local issues. For views of Congress, Richard Fenno Jr.'s “home style” theory may explain the differences.
