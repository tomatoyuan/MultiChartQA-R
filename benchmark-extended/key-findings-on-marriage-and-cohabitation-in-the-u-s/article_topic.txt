This article presents key findings from a Pew Research Center study on marriage and cohabitation in the U.S. It notes that as marriage rates have declined, the share of adults who have cohabited has risen, with more 18 - 44 - year - olds having cohabited than married. Most Americans find cohabitation acceptable, even without marriage plans, though a narrow majority sees societal benefits in marriage. Married adults report higher relationship satisfaction and trust than cohabiters. Many cohabiters view living together as a step towards marriage, with more educated non - engaged cohabiters more likely to hold this view. Finances and convenience are major reasons for cohabitation, while love and companionship top the list for both marriage and cohabitation. Non - engaged cohabiters who want to marry often cite financial and career - related reasons for not being engaged. Finally, about two - thirds of Americans favor giving unmarried couples the same legal rights as married couples, with significant party differences in views on related issues.
