This article delves into various aspects of U.S. foreign aid. It starts by highlighting the Trump administration's moves to freeze foreign aid and shutter the main aid - distributing agency, which has created uncertainty. It reveals that the federal government is projected to spend about $58.4 billion on international assistance in the 2025 fiscal year, though this may change. In 2023, $71.9 billion was disbursed, making up 1.2% of total federal outlays. U.S. foreign aid is used for diverse purposes like economic development, disaster relief, combating diseases, and promoting democracy. Ukraine was the largest recipient in 2023. The U.S. Agency for International Development (USAID) is the primary aid - distributing agency but is being dismantled by the Trump administration. Military assistance includes designated military aid and large - scale foreign military sales and direct commercial sales. Americans have mixed views on foreign aid, with a split on increasing or decreasing spending, and they prioritize domestic - related foreign policy goals over promoting democracy and human rights abroad, despite foreign aid being directed towards some of these priorities.
