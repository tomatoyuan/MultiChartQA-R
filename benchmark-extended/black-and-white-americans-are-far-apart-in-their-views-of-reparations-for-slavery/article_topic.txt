This article explores the significant racial, ethnic, and partisan divides in Americans' views on reparations for slavery. Discussions about atonement for Black enslavement have a long - standing history in the US, with some recent efforts like Evanston's reparations plan, California's task - force, and Harvard's fund. A 2021 Pew Research Center survey shows that overall, Americans view reparations negatively, with only 30% supporting it. However, around 77% of Black adults support reparations compared to 18% of White adults. Partisan differences are also notable, with 48% of Democrats and Democratic - leaners in favor compared to only 8% of Republicans and GOP leaners. Younger adults are more likely to support reparations than older ones. There are also differences in views on the legacy of slavery's impact on Black Americans, with 85% of Black Americans, 64% of Hispanics, and 50% of Whites saying it affects Black people at least a fair amount. Among reparations supporters, the federal government is seen as most responsible, and educational scholarships are considered the most helpful form of repayment. Most supporters of reparations think it's unlikely to happen in their lifetime, with some demographic differences in this view.
