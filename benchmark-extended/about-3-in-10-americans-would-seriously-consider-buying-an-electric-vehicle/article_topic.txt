This article from Pew Research Center analyzes Americans' views on electric vehicles (EVs). It notes that while EV sales have reached record highs, growth in the US has slowed and only about three - in - ten Americans would seriously consider buying an EV, down 9 percentage points in a year. The article explores factors influencing the choice between EVs and gas - powered vehicles, including environmental benefits, cost, driving experience, and reliability. It reveals that while nearly half think EVs are better for the environment, this share has dropped 20 points since 2021. Most believe EVs require a bigger up - front investment, and opinions on charging costs are split. Only 13% find EVs more fun to drive, and half think they're less reliable. Democrats view EVs more favorably than Republicans on all dimensions. Additionally, more Americans are likely to consider a hybrid (43%) than an EV (29%). Concerns about charging infrastructure are significant, with 56% not confident the US will build enough for large numbers of EVs, and this lack of confidence is strongly tied to lower interest in purchasing EVs.
