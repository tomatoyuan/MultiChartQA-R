This article, based on a Pew Research Center analysis of 2020 census data, reveals that U.S. household growth in the 2010s was the lowest on record, with only 9% growth compared to the prior record - low of 11% in the previous decade. The number of new households added was fewer than in any decade from 1950 - 2010. Despite economic expansion after the 2007 - 2009 recession, household growth did not increase as expected. Subpar household growth impacts the broader economy, and several factors contribute to it, including slow population growth, an increase in multigenerational family living, the demographic makeup of fast - growing racial/ethnic groups, a decline in adults living alone, geographic trends (more people in metropolitan areas), and rising housing costs. Although experts expected an increase in household size, it actually decreased from 2010 to 2020. Household growth varied across states, with some having double - digit growth and others experiencing little growth or decline.
