The article, based on a Pew Research Center survey of 9,676 U.S. adults in October 2021, reveals that 55% of U.S. adults live within an hour’s drive of at least some extended family members. It explores various factors influencing proximity to extended family, such as education (those with higher education are less likely to live close), income (lower - and middle - income adults are more likely to live near family), race/ethnicity (Asian Americans are less likely to live near family), region (Northeast and Midwest adults are more likely), community type (rural adults are more likely), age (50 - 64 - year - olds are more likely), household type (multigenerational and “sandwich generation” adults are more likely). An overwhelming majority of Americans value having family close, with women and those 65 and older more likely to say it’s very important. Living near extended family also has economic impacts, like helping married women with young children enter the workforce and reducing medical expenses through informal caregiving.
