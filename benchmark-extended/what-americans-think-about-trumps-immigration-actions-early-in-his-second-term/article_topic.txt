This article from Pew Research Center explores what Americans think about Trump's immigration actions early in his second term. It reveals that about half of Americans say the administration is doing the “right amount” on deportations, but almost as many think it's doing “too much,” and few say “too little.” A majority approve of increased deportations and a greater military presence at the U.S.-Mexico border, while cutting federal funds to non - cooperating cities/states and suspending asylum applications are less popular. There are wide partisan differences, with Republicans generally approving of Trump's immigration agenda and Democrats disapproving. Racial and ethnic groups also show varying levels of support, with White adults generally more supportive, Black adults the least, and Asian Americans more supportive than Hispanic adults, and partisan divides exist within each racial and ethnic group.
