This article, based on a Pew Research Center survey of 10,260 U.S. adults from Nov. 1 - 7, 2021, examines the age - related differences in Americans' views on driverless cars. Older adults (50 and above) are more wary than younger adults (18 - 49). A larger share of those 50 and older believe the widespread use of driverless cars is bad for society, would increase traffic casualties, and are less likely to want to ride in one or share the road with them. Younger adults are more likely to favor applying driverless technology to various vehicles. There are also age differences regarding the perceived consequences of widespread use: older adults are more concerned about hacking risks, while younger adults are more likely to see positive outcomes like easier travel and greater independence for the elderly and disabled. When it comes to job losses, there's no significant age - related difference. Older adults are also more likely to support additional safety precautions for driverless cars.
