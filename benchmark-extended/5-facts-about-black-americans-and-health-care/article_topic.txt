This article, based on a 2021 Pew Research Center survey of 14,497 U.S. adults including 3,546 Black adults, explores Black Americans' attitudes and experiences with healthcare. It reveals that while most Black Americans think health outcomes for Black people have improved in the past 20 years and a majority report positive recent healthcare experiences, they also have structural concerns and face disparities like higher cancer and maternal mortality rates. Around 61% rate their recent care as excellent or very good, but views vary by income. A majority (55%) have had at least one negative interaction with providers, with younger Black women most likely to report such experiences and to prefer Black providers. Lack of access to high - quality care is cited as the top reason for worse health outcomes among Black people, and most Black Americans say it doesn't matter if their provider is Black, yet Black providers are underrepresented in the medical field.
