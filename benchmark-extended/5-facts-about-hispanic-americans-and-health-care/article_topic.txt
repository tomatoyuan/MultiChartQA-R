This article presents five key facts about Hispanic Americans and health care based on a 2021 Pew Research Center survey and other sources. Hispanic adults are less likely than other Americans to see a health - care provider recently and have a primary care provider, and access varies among immigrants based on length of U.S. residency. They also have a higher uninsured rate than other racial and ethnic groups, though it has declined since 2010. Many Hispanic adults attribute worse health outcomes to occupational and structural factors, and the coronavirus outbreak hit them hard. A third prefer Spanish - speaking or Hispanic health - care providers, with immigrants more likely to hold this preference. Hispanic Americans are underrepresented in the health - care workforce, and Black Hispanic adults are more likely to report negative health - care experiences, yet most Hispanic adults have positive opinions of their latest health - care interaction.
