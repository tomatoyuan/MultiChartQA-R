This article, written to mark Disability Pride Month in the United States, presents eight facts about Americans with disabilities based on government data and Pew Research Center surveys. It notes that there are about 42.5 million Americans with disabilities, 13% of the civilian non - institutionalized population. Older Americans, American Indians and Alaska Natives are more likely to have disabilities. The most common disabilities involve walking, independent living, or cognition. Disabled Americans generally earn less, have lower technology adoption rates for some devices, and are less likely to use the internet daily. The percentage of public school students receiving special education has increased. They also faced more psychological distress during the COVID - 19 pandemic. Additionally, most non - fully remote employed Americans think their workplace is at least somewhat accessible for physical disabilities, and half of workers highly value workplace physical accessibility regardless of disability status.
