The article, based on a 2016 Pew Research Center survey of Hispanic adults, reveals that Hispanics are more likely than the general U.S. public to believe in core parts of the American dream, such as hard - work paying off and each successive generation being better off. However, many Latinos find the American dream hard to achieve, with only about half saying they've achieved it and three - quarters stating it's difficult for people like them. Their life goals, including being a good parent and providing for the family, overlap with aspects of the American dream. Among those who achieved the dream, they attributed it to homeownership, financial stability, work, or coming to the U.S. Those yet to achieve it hope to do so through homeownership, work, or education. Latinos also worry about providing for their family and finances. Additionally, belief in intergenerational progress fades as their immigrant roots grow distant, with lower percentages of third - or higher - generation Latinos expecting to be better off than their parents and for their children to be better off.
