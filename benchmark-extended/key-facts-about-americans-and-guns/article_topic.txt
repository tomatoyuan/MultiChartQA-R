This article presents key findings from Pew Research Center surveys about Americans' relationships with guns. It notes that guns are deeply ingrained in American society and politics, with the Second Amendment guaranteeing the right to bear arms and about a third of adults owning a gun. Measuring gun ownership has challenges, but surveys show relatively stable rates over time, with differences by factors like political affiliation, gender, community type, and race/ethnicity. Personal protection is the top reason for gun ownership. Gun owners and non - owners in gun households have different feelings about guns, and non - gun owners are split on future gun ownership. A majority of Americans think it's too easy to legally obtain a gun and favor stricter gun laws. There's bipartisan support for some gun policies, but most are politically divisive. The public is closely divided on protecting gun rights versus controlling gun ownership, and views also vary on whether gun ownership increases or decreases safety, the impact of more guns on society, and the severity of gun violence as a problem. Additionally, many K - 12 teachers and parents are worried about school shootings.
