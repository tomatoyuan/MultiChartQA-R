This article, based on a Pew Research Center survey of 10,329 U.S. adults from May 30 - June 4, 2023, explores Americans' views on electric vehicles (EVs). About 38% are likely to consider an EV for their next purchase, a 4 - point drop from 2022. Democrats, younger adults, urban residents, and current hybrid/EV owners are more open to EVs, with helping the environment and saving on gas being major reasons. The availability of public charging stations is a potential obstacle, and Americans have limited confidence in building the necessary infrastructure, with Republicans being more skeptical. Those confident in infrastructure development are more likely to consider an EV. The idea of phasing out new gas - powered vehicles by 2035 faces more opposition (59%) than support, and support has declined since 2021 among both Democrats and Republicans, with partisans having different emotional reactions to the concept.
