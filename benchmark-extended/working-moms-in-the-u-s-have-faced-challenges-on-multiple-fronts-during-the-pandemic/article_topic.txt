This article, based on Pew Research Center surveys, explores the challenges U.S. working moms faced during the COVID - 19 pandemic. In the early months, the share of moms preferring not to work for pay increased, and the preference for full - time work decreased. Employed moms were more likely than dads to encounter professional hurdles, such as not being able to give 100% at work, reducing work hours due to parenting, and being seen as less committed. Mothers generally shoulder more child - care duties than their spouses, a pattern that predates the pandemic. Many working moms found it difficult to handle child - care responsibilities, especially during the early stages of the outbreak. A significant portion of moms teleworking had a lot of child - care duties at home, and both moms and dads faced difficulties getting work done without interruptions. In 2020, around six - in - ten moms felt they spent the right amount of time with their children, while nearly half of dads said they spent too little time.
