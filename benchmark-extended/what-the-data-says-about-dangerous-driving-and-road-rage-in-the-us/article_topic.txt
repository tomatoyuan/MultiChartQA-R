This article, based on a Pew Research Center survey and data from various sources such as the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention, National Highway Traffic Safety Administration, and Gun Violence Archive, explores dangerous driving and road rage in the U.S. About half of Americans perceive people in their area are driving more dangerously post - pandemic, and available data shows this perception may be valid in part. Motor vehicle fatalities rose during the pandemic and then slightly declined in 2022, with Arizona and New Mexico having the largest increases in age - adjusted death rates from 2019 to 2022. Distracted driving, though the percentage in all traffic crashes decreased from 2019 to 2022, still concerns 78% of Americans regarding cellphone use. Reckless driving indicators like hit - and - run and speeding in fatal crashes have increased in some cases. Drunk driving fatalities and charges are up from 2019, reversing a downward trend. Road rage incidents involving firearms, while the number of reported incidents peaked in 2019, have seen an increase in the number of people killed or injured, with the toll peaking in 2022.
