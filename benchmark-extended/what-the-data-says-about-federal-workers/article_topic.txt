This article delves into the federal workforce in light of President - elect Donald Trump's plans to shrink and reshape it for his second administration. It provides data - based answers to common questions about the federal workforce. As of November 2024, the federal government employed over 3 million civilians, excluding 1.3 million active - duty military. The number of non - Postal Service federal workers has grown steadily over time, while the Postal Service's workforce has declined. The Department of Veterans Affairs and the Social Security Administration are among the largest employers. Fewer than a fifth of federal workers are in the Washington, D.C. area. Demographically, federal workers are slightly more male, older, more educated, and have a different racial and ethnic composition compared to the overall workforce. Most are white - collar workers, with health - related fields being the largest occupational category. The average federal worker earns $106,382 annually, and job tenure varies by agency. Most federal workers have civil service protections, but there are exceptions. Public opinion shows mixed feelings towards federal employees and agencies, with positive views of some like the National Park Service and Postal Service, and more negative or mixed views of others like the IRS and CIA.
