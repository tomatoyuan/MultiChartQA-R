This Pew Research Center survey ahead of World Press Freedom Day on May 3 examines Americans’ perceptions of press freedom in the United States. A large majority (73%) of U.S. adults view press freedom as extremely or very important to society, with demographic differences like White and Asian Americans and those with more education and higher income being more likely to hold this view. While most (79%) believe the media are at least somewhat free to report, only a third say they’re completely free, and large majorities think news organizations are influenced by financial and political interests. 41% of Americans are extremely or very concerned about potential restrictions on press freedoms, and journalists are especially worried. Partisan differences exist, with Republicans more likely to express concerns about press freedom and believe political and financial interests have a great deal of influence. When it comes to the tension between protecting press freedom and preventing misinformation, about half say false - information publication should be prevented even if it limits press freedom, while 46% say press freedom should always be protected, with men, White Americans, college graduates, and Republicans more likely to support the latter.
