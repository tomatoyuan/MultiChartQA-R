The article focuses on the growing trend of multigenerational living among young adults (ages 25 - 34) in the United States over the past 50 years. In 2021, 25% of this age - group lived in such households, up from 9% in 1971, with the increase being fastest among this age bracket compared to others. It's more prevalent among those without a college degree, likely due to financial pressures as they earn less. Living in a parent's home is the most common arrangement, with 68% of young adults in multigenerational households doing so in 2021, though other arrangements also contribute to the growth. Most young adults in these households (86%) have a parent in the home, and living with two parents is associated with better economic outcomes, including higher median household income and lower poverty rates. The typical young adult in a multigenerational household contributed 22% of the total income in 2021, with the contribution varying based on who heads the household.
