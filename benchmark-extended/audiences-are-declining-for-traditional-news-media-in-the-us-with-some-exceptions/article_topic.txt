This article from Pew Research Center explores the trends in the U.S. news industry. It reveals that a declining share of U.S. adults closely follow news, and audiences are shrinking for traditional news media like local TV stations, most newspapers, and public radio, while growing for newer platforms such as podcasts and some specific media brands. Daily newspaper circulation is mostly in decline, though big brands like The New York Times and The Wall Street Journal see digital growth. Digital traffic to newspapers’ and top digital news websites is falling. Local TV news audiences have dropped across time slots, with mixed trends for cable and network TV news. In audio, terrestrial radio and NPR listenership is down, but podcast audiences are rising. Economically, the newspaper industry and ABC, CBS, and NBC news programming face advertising revenue losses, while local TV advertising revenue is stable due to digital and political ads, local public radio revenue is increasing, and Fox News leads in cable TV news revenue growth.
