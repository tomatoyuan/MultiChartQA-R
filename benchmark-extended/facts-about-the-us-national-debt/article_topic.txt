This article discusses the U.S. national debt in the context of the ongoing stand - off between President Joe Biden and the Republican - controlled House over raising the debt limit. Public concern about federal spending and reducing the budget deficit is rising, with Republicans more likely to view deficit - cutting as a priority. The U.S. is one of the few countries with a specific monetary limit on its national debt, which emerged in 1939. As of February 10, the total public debt is nearly $31.46 trillion, most of which is subject to the limit. The debt - to - GDP ratio has been high for years, with three main growth phases in recent decades. Intragovernmental entities own 21.8% of the debt, with Social Security holding a large portion. The Federal Reserve is the largest single holder of U.S. government debt, with its holdings increasing significantly during the pandemic. Servicing the debt is a major federal expense, and while the debt service as a share of federal outlays has decreased due to falling interest rates, rates are now rising.
