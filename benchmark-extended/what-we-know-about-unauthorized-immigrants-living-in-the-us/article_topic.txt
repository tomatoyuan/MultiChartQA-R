This article from Pew Research Center presents estimates of the unauthorized immigrant population in the U.S. based on the 2022 American Community Survey, indicating it grew to 11.0 million in 2022, reversing a long - term downward trend from 2007 - 2019 but still below the 2007 peak. It details the “residual method” used for the estimates and defines unauthorized immigrants. Over 3 million unauthorized immigrants have temporary protection from deportation, accounting for nearly 30% of the 2022 estimate. Since July 2022, the population likely increased with record border encounters, new arrivals through programs like CHNV and U4U, and growing asylum backlogs. Key findings show a drop in Mexican unauthorized immigrants, growth from other regions, population changes in states, an increase in unauthorized immigrant workers, and details about their composition, households they live in, countries of origin, and labor force participation. The article also notes that a December 2024 Census Bureau data release may affect these estimates and the center is working on updates.
