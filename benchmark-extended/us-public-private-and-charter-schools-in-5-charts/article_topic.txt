This article, “U.S. public, private and charter schools in 5 charts,” explores the differences between traditional public, private, and charter schools in the United States using data from the National Center for Education Statistics. It details their funding, curriculum flexibility, and enrollment policies, highlighting that traditional public schools are taxpayer - funded and follow district or state standards, private schools charge tuition and have more autonomy, and charter schools are taxpayer - funded but have more flexibility through charters with the government. The article also examines school size and location, showing public schools are larger and more evenly distributed, while charter and private schools are mostly in urban or suburban areas. It looks at enrollment trends over the last decade, with public school enrollment declining, charter school popularity growing, and private school enrollment relatively steady. State - level enrollment shares vary, and charter schools are more racially and ethnically diverse, with their students more likely to qualify for the National School Lunch Program. Finally, it compares the teaching staff across the three school types in terms of race, age, experience, and educational attainment.
