This article from Pew Research Center analyzes how Republicans view climate change and energy issues. Few Republicans see climate change as a top - priority for the country and express limited concern about its impact on the U.S., in contrast to Democrats. However, they support some climate - related proposals like sealing methane leaks and carbon - capture tax credits, while opposing more aggressive ones. Republicans emphasize economic factors in climate policies and support expanding both fossil fuel and renewable energy sources but prioritize fossil fuels. They're concerned about a transition to renewable energy, doubting its reliability and fearing price increases, and are skeptical about electric vehicles. GOP views on renewable energy have become more negative since 2020. There are age differences within the GOP, with younger Republicans more likely to link human activity to climate change and less supportive of fossil fuels. A minority of Republicans say climate change affects their community, and they're less likely than Democrats to report extreme weather, but those who do often connect it to climate change.
