This article from Pew Research Center analyzes veterans working in the federal government. As of September 2024, about 713,000 veterans were employed by the federal government, making up nearly a quarter of the civilian workforce, compared to just 5% of all employed Americans. The share of veterans in non - Postal Service federal jobs has been fairly steady over 20 years, with a high of 31.6% in 2016 - 2017 and a low of 27.8% in 2024, and 10% - 11% in the Postal Service in the past five years. The Defense Department and its military departments, along with some smaller military - connected agencies, employ large shares of veterans, but they also make up significant portions of agencies without direct military links. Veterans work in a wide range of occupations, predominating in areas like armaments and equipment maintenance. When compared to non - veterans in the federal workforce, veterans tend to be older, more likely to be men, less likely to have a college education, and have a slightly shorter average length of service of 25 years or more, though their average federal civilian employment length is slightly longer. Racial and ethnic differences could not be analyzed due to unavailable data.
