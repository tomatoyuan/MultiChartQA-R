This article from Pew Research Center focuses on the gender pay gap in the U.S. Over the past 20 years, the gap has slightly narrowed, with women in 2024 earning an average of 85% of men's earnings compared to 81% in 2003, and it's even smaller among 25 - 34 - year - olds. The long - term narrowing is due in part to women's gains in education, occupation, and work experience, though women still tend to be overrepresented in lower - paying jobs. The U.S. Census Bureau's analysis of full - time workers shows similar trends. A 2022 survey reveals that half of U.S. adults believe employers treating women differently is a major reason for the gap, with differences in views based on gender, parental status, and political party. Working women, especially mothers, face more pressure regarding home responsibilities, while there are similarities in financial support and career success pressures. Additionally, men are more likely to be bosses or top managers and less likely to say they don't want such positions in the future compared to women.
