The article, based on a Pew Research Center survey as part of the Pew - Knight Initiative, explores Americans' sources of local news. It reveals that while the way Americans consume local news is changing, with more getting news online and fewer using daily newspapers, friends, family, and neighbors remain the most common source of local news, ranking above TV, radio, and online forums. About 73% of Americans often or sometimes get local news from them. Local news exchanges among these groups still mainly occur via word - of - mouth, though the share getting news via social media has increased. Friends, family, and neighbors are top sources for local politics and crime news, and social interaction is a common way to respond to local crime news. Most people getting local crime news from them think it's at least somewhat accurate, and over half believe their friends and family get the amount of crime about right.
