This Pew Research Center analysis focuses on how Mexicans and Americans view each other and their governments’ handling of the U.S.-Mexico border situation. It reveals that currently, most Mexicans (61%) have a favorable view of the U.S., while 60% of Americans have an unfavorable view of Mexico, a reversal from 2017. Views differ by age, race, ethnicity, and party in both countries, with younger people generally more positive towards the other nation. Americans are more critical than Mexicans of both the U.S. and Mexican governments’ handling of the border. Higher education and income are associated with more negative views of the governments’ border - handling in both countries, and Republicans in the U.S. are more likely to hold negative views than Democrats. Mexicans’ confidence in U.S. presidents has shifted, with low confidence in both Trump and Biden, and generally little confidence in other world leaders surveyed.
