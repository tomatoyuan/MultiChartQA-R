This article, based on Pew Research Center surveys in March and April 2018 after the Parkland high - school shooting, reveals that a majority of U.S. teens (57%) and most parents (63%) are at least somewhat worried about a shooting at school. Non - white teens, girls, non - white parents, mothers, and lower - income parents express higher levels of concern. When it comes to preventing school shootings, teens think measures like preventing mentally ill people from buying guns, improving mental health screening, having metal detectors, and banning assault - style weapons are more effective, while allowing teachers to carry guns is seen as effective by a much smaller share. Views on these measures vary by race, with black teens having different opinions from white and Hispanic teens. Among adults, there is a partisan divide on arming teachers and banning assault - style weapons, but both Democrats and Republicans agree on the potential effectiveness of mental - illness - directed proposals and metal detectors.
