The article presents key facts about Asian Americans living in poverty. It notes that while Asian Americans are often seen as successful, about one - in - ten (over 2.3 million) lived in poverty in 2022, with rates varying widely across origin groups like high rates for Burmese and Hmong Americans and low rates for Filipinos and Indian Americans. A third of Asian Americans aged 25 and older in poverty have a bachelor's degree, and most are immigrants with limited English proficiency. Around 1 million Asians below the poverty line live in 10 U.S. metro areas, with over half in New York, Los Angeles, and San Francisco. Many in poverty face financial challenges such as inability to save, bill - paying trouble, and getting food from charities. They often turn to family, friends, or governments for help, and immigration reasons are linked to government assistance. Views on the American dream vary by poverty status, but both groups have similar ideas about what achieving it entails, though homeownership, seen as important, is less common among those in poverty.
