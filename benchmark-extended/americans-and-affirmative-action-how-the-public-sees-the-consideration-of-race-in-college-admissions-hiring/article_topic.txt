The article delves into Americans' views on affirmative action, a long - standing concept in the U.S. originating from a 1961 executive order. It explains that affirmative action aims to enhance educational and employment opportunities for historically discriminated racial and ethnic minorities but has sparked debates. Based on Pew Research Center and Gallup surveys, around 79% of U.S. adults have heard of the term, with college graduates, higher - income individuals, and older people being more likely to be familiar. Public attitudes towards affirmative action vary depending on how it's presented; for example, 36% of those familiar with the term saw it as good, while 29% saw it as bad. More Americans disapprove than approve of colleges considering race and ethnicity in admissions and companies doing so in hiring and promotions, though they generally value workplace diversity. Racial and ethnic minorities, especially Black Americans, are more likely to support race - based considerations than White Americans, and Democrats are far more likely than Republicans to approve of such considerations in both college admissions and hiring.
