This article analyzes crime in the U.S. using data from the FBI, Bureau of Justice Statistics, Pew Research Center, and Gallup. It reveals that while crime rates, especially violent and property crimes, have declined since the 1990s, there have been recent spikes, like the 2020 increase in the murder rate. Property crime is more common than violent crime, with larceny/theft and aggravated assault being the most prevalent in each category. Americans generally perceive crime as increasing, despite official data showing a decrease, and attitudes vary by party, race, and ethnicity. Some demographics are more likely to be crime victims, and there are significant geographic differences in crime rates. Most crimes are not reported to police, and most reported crimes are not solved, with clearance rates at their lowest since 1993.
