The article, based on a Pew Research Center survey of 5,123 U.S. adults from Feb. 24 - March 2, 2025, reveals that since the updated flu and COVID - 19 vaccines became available last summer, more U.S. adults (42%) reported getting the flu shot than the updated COVID - 19 shot (27%), similar to last year's uptake levels. Over half of U.S. adults (53%) got neither vaccine, while 22% got both. Democrats are far more likely than Republicans to get each vaccine, with the gap especially wide among those 65 and older for the COVID - 19 vaccine. The share of Americans getting the updated COVID - 19 vaccine is largely unchanged from a year ago and much lower than earlier in the pandemic, as fewer view COVID - 19 as a major threat and hospitalization and death rates have declined. The CDC recommends both vaccines to protect against severe illness.
