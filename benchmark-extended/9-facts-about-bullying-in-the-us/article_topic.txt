This article presents 9 facts about bullying in the U.S. based on Pew Research Center surveys and federal data sources. It shows that bullying is a top concern for U.S. parents, with about a third extremely or very worried about their children being bullied. Around half of U.S. teens view online harassment as a major problem, and nearly half have experienced cyberbullying, with offensive name - calling being the most common type. Older teen girls, Black and Hispanic teens, and those from lower - income households are more likely to face certain types of online bullying. In the 2019 - 2020 school year, about two - in - ten middle and high school students were bullied at school, with the classroom being the most common location, and girls, middle schoolers, and rural students were more likely to be victims. Less than half of bullied students notified an adult, and younger and more frequently bullied students were more likely to do so. In 2021, high schoolers who are gay, lesbian, bisexual, questioning or identify in some other way were about twice as likely as heterosexual students to be bullied both at school and online.
