This article, based on a 2022 Pew Research Center survey of 1,316 U.S. teens aged 13 - 17 and their parents, delves into American teens' social - media experiences. It reveals that YouTube is the most commonly used platform among teens, followed by TikTok, Instagram, and Snapchat, with significant shifts in platform usage since 2014 - 15, such as the rise of TikTok and decline of Facebook. Usage varies by race, ethnicity, and gender. Many teens use YouTube and TikTok daily and some use them almost constantly. More than half find it hard to give up social media, and girls and older teens are more likely to feel this way. Teens perceive social media's impact differently on themselves and others, reporting more positive than negative experiences, though there are gender differences in these experiences. Many teens think criminal charges or permanent bans would help reduce social - media abuse. Some, especially older girls, avoid posting due to various concerns. Teens generally feel they have little control over data collection but are not overly worried. Most teens don't think their parents are highly worried about their social - media use, and many see a disconnect between parental views and their own experiences. Parents are most worried about their teens being exposed to explicit content on social media.
