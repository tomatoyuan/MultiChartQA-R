The article, based on Pew Research Center analysis of CDC data, reveals that gun deaths among U.S. children and teens under 18 increased 50% from 2019 (1,732) to 2021 (2,590), with the rate rising 46% from 2.4 to 3.5 per 100,000, the highest since at least 1999. This is part of a broader increase in overall U.S. firearm deaths. In 2021, homicides accounted for 60% of child and teen gun - deaths, followed by suicides at 32% and accidents at 5%, different from adults where suicides were the majority. There were also over 11,000 estimated non - fatal gun - related emergency - room visits in 2020. Gun deaths are more common among boys, older children, and certain racial and ethnic groups, like Black children and teens who were five times as likely as White counterparts to die from gunfire. A fall 2022 survey showed that nearly half of U.S. parents worry about their children getting shot, with demographic and partisan differences in these concerns.
