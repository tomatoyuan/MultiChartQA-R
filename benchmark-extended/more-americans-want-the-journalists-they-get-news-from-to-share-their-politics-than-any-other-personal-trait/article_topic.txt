The article, based on a 2023 Pew Research Center survey of 10,701 U.S. adults, explores Americans' views on the importance of journalists sharing their personal traits. It finds that while most Americans say it's unimportant for journalists to share their political views, age, gender, etc., more people consider it important for journalists to share their politics than any other trait, with about four - in - ten Americans holding this view. Opinions vary by demographic groups: politically, conservative Republicans and liberal Democrats are more likely to want news from journalists who share their politics; younger adults are more inclined to want news from journalists of a similar age and gender; Black Americans are more likely to want news from journalists who share their race or ethnicity; and religiously, those identifying with a religion, especially White evangelicals and Black Protestants, are more likely to want journalists with shared religious views.
