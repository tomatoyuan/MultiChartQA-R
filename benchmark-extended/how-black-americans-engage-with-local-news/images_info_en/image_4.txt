1. Among U.S. Black adults who feel extremely/very attached to their local community, the proportion who say they see or hear news coverage about their local community extremely/fairly often is 52%.
2. Among U.S. Black adults who feel somewhat attached to their local community, the proportion who say they see or hear news coverage about their local community extremely/fairly often is 38%.
3. Among U.S. Black adults who feel not too/not at all attached to their local community, the proportion who say they see or hear news coverage about their local community extremely/fairly often is 26%.
4. Among U.S. Black adults who feel extremely/very attached to their local community, the proportion who say local journalists in their area are mostly in touch with their local community is 67%.
5. Among U.S. Black adults who feel somewhat attached to their local community, the proportion who say local journalists in their area are mostly in touch with their local community is 62%.
6. Among U.S. Black adults who feel not too/not at all attached to their local community, the proportion who say local journalists in their area are mostly in touch with their local community is 50%.
7. Among U.S. Black adults who feel extremely/very attached to their local community (and see or hear local news coverage at least sometimes), the proportion who say that on the whole, coverage of their local community is very/somewhat fair is 56%.
8. Among U.S. Black adults who feel somewhat attached to their local community (and see or hear local news coverage at least sometimes), the proportion who say that on the whole, coverage of their local community is very/somewhat fair is 44%.
9. Among U.S. Black adults who feel not too/not at all attached to their local community (and see or hear local news coverage at least sometimes), the proportion who say that on the whole, coverage of their local community is very/somewhat fair is 38%.
The survey period is from Feb. 22 - March 5, 2023.
