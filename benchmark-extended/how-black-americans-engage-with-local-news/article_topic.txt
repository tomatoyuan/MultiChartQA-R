This article, based on a Pew Research Center survey of 4,742 U.S. Black adults, explores how Black Americans engage with local news. It reveals that around three - quarters of Black adults see or hear local news at least sometimes, with just over a third getting it extremely or fairly often, and this is more common among older adults and those in majority - Black communities. Overall, more positive than negative views are expressed towards local journalists and news coverage, with 58% saying journalists are in touch with the community and 44% perceiving coverage as fair. Older adults and Black Democrats tend to have more positive views. Also, 48% trust the accuracy of local news outlets more than other sources. There's no clear consensus on the amount of local news coverage. Additionally, Black Americans more attached to their community are more likely to consume local news and hold positive views, though those in mostly Black communities, while more engaged with local news, are slightly more likely to say local journalists are out of touch.
